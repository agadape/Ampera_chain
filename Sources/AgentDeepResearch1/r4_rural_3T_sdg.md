# Deep Research Report: PLN Indonesia Electrification of 3T Villages and SDG Alignment

## Executive Summary

Indonesia's state electricity company PLN (PT PLN Persero) is executing an accelerated rural electrification campaign targeting the country's "3T" regions—**Tertinggal** (lagging/disadvantaged), **Terdepan** (frontier), and **Terluar** (outermost)—through the **Lisdes (Village Electricity) Program** (2025-2029) and the **Diesel Replacement Program (DRP)**. As of May 2025, Indonesia's national electrification ratio reached **99.8%**, with 83,693 villages electrified by end-2024, yet approximately **658,000 households** remain unelectrified, concentrated in Eastern Indonesia's 3T regions. This report analyzes the technological solutions, pilot projects, community ownership models, and SDG impact metrics aligned with **SDG 7** (Affordable Clean Energy), **SDG 9** (Industry, Innovation, Infrastructure), and **SDG 13** (Climate Action).

---

## 1. Policy Framework and Program Architecture

### 1.1 The 3T Village Classification
Indonesia's 3T regions encompass:
- **Tertinggal**: Economically disadvantaged districts with low human development indices
- **Terdepan**: Border and frontier regions adjacent to other countries
- **Terluar**: Remote outermost islands and archipelagic zones

These areas historically face the "last mile" electrification gap due to geographical isolation, high transmission costs, and limited grid connectivity.

### 1.2 PLN's Lisdes Program (2025-2029)
The flagship **Village Electricity (Lisdes)** program was officially launched in May 2025 with the following targets:
- **10,068 unelectrified villages and hamlets** to be connected by 2029
- **780,000 households** targeted for new electricity connections
- **Investment**: Approximately **Rp 50 trillion** (USD ~3.1 billion)
- **Generation capacity**: **394 MW** of new power plants
- **Service standard**: 24/7 reliable electricity supply

The program prioritizes mountainous areas, islands, and border regions where grid extension is not economically feasible. It operates through collaboration between central/local governments, private sector investors, and development partners.

*Source: [PLN aims to electrify 10,000 villages by 2029](https://tanahair.net/pln-aims-to-electrify-10000-villages-by-2029-targeting-780000-households/)*

### 1.3 Diesel Replacement Program (DRP)
Launched in March 2022, the DRP targets **5,200 diesel power plants (PLTD)** operating in remote areas:
- **2,396 PLTD** across **741 locations** to be converted to solar PV + BESS (3.21 GWp solar, 9.03 GWh storage)
- Total DRP pipeline: **94 locations** with >200 MW capacity in initial tender phases
- Objective: Eliminate diesel dependency, reduce electricity costs, and decarbonize remote generation

*Source: [Powering Indonesia's Sustainable Future: The Diesel Replacement Program](https://energyalliance.org/indonesia-diesel-replacement-program/)*

## 2. Off-Grid and Semi-Off-Grid Technologies

### 2.1 Solar Home Systems (SHS)
Solar Home Systems provide basic electricity services (lighting, phone charging) for households beyond grid reach. Indonesia's SHS market has evolved from World Bank-supported programs in the 1990s to commercial pay-as-you-go (PAYGO) models:
- **Cost**: ~USD 3.50/month over 3 years for basic systems
- **Capacity**: Typically 10-50 Wp for basic lighting and device charging
- **Limitation**: Insufficient for productive uses without significant upgrade

*Source: [Off-Grid Solar Brings Light, Time, Income to Remotest Indonesia Villages](https://www.voanews.com/a/off-grid-solar-brings-light-time-income-to-remotest-indonesia-villages/7092927.html)*

### 2.2 Solar Mini-Grids
Solar mini-grids (100 kWp to 1 MW) serve clusters of households and public facilities:
- **Treweng Village, East Nusa Tenggara**: PLN-installed **110 kWp solar grid** (2020) serving 175+ households, reducing costs from IDR 40,000 to IDR 10,000/month, enabling fish freezing and boat repair businesses
- **Muara Enggelam, East Kalimantan**: **35-45 kWp communal solar grid** managed by Village-Owned Enterprise (VOE), powering swallow's nest farming, schools, and clinics
- **Mata Redi, Sumba**: **60 kWp + 35 kWp AC-coupled solar units** (MENTARI program, 2023) with 10-year battery warranty, serving 220 households and 13 public facilities

*Source: [Community-based Renewable Energy for Eastern Indonesia](https://www.climatepolicyinitiative.org/electrifying-the-last-mile-community-based-renewable-energy-for-eastern-indonesia/)*

### 2.3 Pico-Hydro and Micro-Hydro Systems
Pico-hydro (<5 kW) and micro-hydro (5-100 kW) leverage Indonesia's mountainous terrain and abundant water resources:
- **IBEKA (Indonesian Institute for Energy Development)**: Installed **2,260 kW** across **61 micro/mini-hydro plants** in 82 villages since 1992
- **SITMo (Sistem Informasi Teknologi Mini Hydro)**: Provided electricity to **160+ families across 11 villages** by 2005
- **Productive use integration**: Micro-hydro powers agricultural processing (rice milling, coffee drying) and small enterprises
- **Geographic focus**: Papua, Maluku, Sulawesi, and Sumatra highlands where solar irradiation is lower

*Source: [IBEKA: Community Owned Electrical Mini-Grids in Indonesia](https://participedia.net/case/ibeka-community-owned-and-managed-mini-grids-in-indonesia)*

## 3. Productive Use of Energy (PUE)

Productive Use of Energy transforms electricity access into income-generating activities:

### 3.1 Agricultural and Livelihood Applications
- **Treweng**: Fish freezing (IDR 10,000/month vs. previous IDR 40,000), fishing boat repair
- **Muara Enggelam**: Swallow's nest farming with sound systems, reducing energy costs from IDR 80,000 to IDR 50,000/month
- **Mata Redi**: Ginger and lemongrass cultivation, wood processing, candlenut processing, women's farmer groups (5 groups formed)
- **EnDev/GIZ PUE Pilot**: 53 businesses across 9 villages in Sumatra and Sulawesi

### 3.2 Economic Impact Metrics
- Household energy cost reduction: **50-75%** compared to diesel generation
- New enterprise formation: **20 BUMDes** launched diverse businesses under UNDP-KOICA ACCESS
- Revenue generation: IDR 11 million/month (Lengora Pantai), IDR 7 million/month (Tasipi)
- Women's economic empowerment: 49% of Renewable Energy Service Company (UPLD) officers are women

*Source: [Productive Use of Energy for Micro-hydro Power in Indonesia](https://energypedia.info/wiki/Productive_Use_of_Energy_for_Micro-hydro_Power_in_Indonesia)*

## 4. Diesel Substitution and De-Dieselization

### 4.1 Scale of Diesel Dependency
- **5,200 PLTD** units across **2,130 locations** with ~2,000 MW total capacity
- High operational costs due to diesel logistics in remote areas
- Environmental impact: Significant CO2 emissions and local air pollution

### 4.2 Conversion Technologies
The DRP employs:
- **Solar PV + BESS**: Primary replacement technology for <100 kW systems
- **Hybrid solar-diesel**: Transitional solution for higher loads
- **Grid integration**: Where technically feasible, connecting remote micro-grids to the main PLN grid

### 4.3 Financial and Operational Models
- **GEAPP support**: Bridging finance for project initiation
- **International partnerships**: ib vogt GmbH (Germany), InfraCo Asia
- **Tender structure**: 52 renewable energy projects across Indonesia (PLN-GEAPP auction platform)

*Source: [PLN to Swap 2,396 Diesel Power Plants with 3.21 GWp Solar](https://danantaramonitor.org/pln-to-swap-2396-diesel-power-plants-with-3-21-gwp-solar-power-plants-and-9-03-gwh-bess/)*

## 5. Community-Owned Energy Models

### 5.1 Village-Owned Enterprises (BUMDes)
BUMDes serve as the primary institutional vehicle for community energy management:
- **Legal framework**: Indonesian Village Law mandates BUMDes as economic development entities
- **Muara Enggelam model**: VOE manages 45 kWp solar grid, collects fees (IDR 50,000/month), cross-subsidizes low-income households, funds maintenance
- **Governance**: Monthly financial disclosure at community meetings, transparent tariff structures

### 5.2 IBEKA Community Hydro Model
- **Community ownership**: Villagers hold equity in hydro projects
- **Capacity building**: Local technicians trained for O&M
- **Revenue sharing**: Tariffs set to cover O&M with surplus for community development
- **Scale**: 61 plants, 2,260 kW total capacity

### 5.3 Challenges in Community Management
- **Capacity gaps**: Limited technical skills for O&M (Watukarere case: grid operating at 50% capacity)
- **Payment collection**: Low-income communities require flexible payment schemes
- **Asset stranding**: PLN's stringent SOE asset acquisition standards prevent transfer of functional off-grid assets when grid arrives (Treweng case)

*Source: [Community-based Renewable Energy for Eastern Indonesia](https://www.climatepolicyinitiative.org/electrifying-the-last-mile-community-based-renewable-energy-for-eastern-indonesia/)*

## 6. Remaining Un-Electrified Villages

### 6.1 Current Status (2025-2026)
- **National electrification ratio**: 99.8% (May 2025), 99.83% (Q1 2026)
- **Households without electricity**: ~658,000 families (primarily in Eastern Indonesia)
- **Villages without electricity**: ~140 completely unelectrified; ~11,000 targeted for 2025-2029 Lisdes
- **Geographic concentration**: East Nusa Tenggara (89.22% electrification), Papua (47.69%), West Papua (89.94%), Maluku (87.02%)

### 6.2 Barriers to Final Electrification
- **Terrain**: Mountainous areas and small islands increase grid extension costs
- **Population density**: Scattered settlements make mini-grids more economical than grid extension
- **Financial sustainability**: Low population density reduces revenue base for O&M
- **Diesel dependency**: Over 2,000 locations still reliant on PLTD

*Source: [Why Community-Based Energy Transition is Indonesia's Answer](https://madaniberkelanjutan.id/news-event/658-000-families-without-electricity-why-community-based-energy-transition-is-indonesia-s-answer)*

## 10. Challenges and Knowledge Gaps

### 10.1 Technical Challenges
- **Grid vs. off-grid tension**: PLN's stringent asset standards create stranded assets when grid arrives (Treweng case)
- **O&M capacity**: Limited local technical skills for mini-grid maintenance
- **Battery lifecycle**: 5-7 year replacement cycles for BESS in remote areas

### 10.2 Financial Sustainability
- **Tariff affordability**: Balancing cost recovery with ability to pay in low-income communities
- **Subsidy dependency**: Many projects require ongoing external funding for O&M
- **Payment collection**: Low population density increases per-capita collection costs

### 10.3 Institutional Coordination
- **Fragmented programs**: Multiple ministries (ESDM, Villages, Environment) with overlapping mandates
- **Data gaps**: Inconsistent tracking of 3T-specific electrification metrics
- **Private sector participation**: Limited commercial interest in low-return remote projects

## 11. Recommendations for Accelerated SDG Alignment

### 11.1 SDG 7 Acceleration
1. **Standardize 3T definitions** across ministries for targeted resource allocation
2. **Scale PAYGO SHS** with battery leasing models for productive uses
3. **Integrate mini-grids with main grid** planning to avoid stranded assets

### 11.2 SDG 9 Innovation
1. **Establish 3T technology incubators** for locally-appropriate solutions
2. **Create diesel replacement market signals** through standardized PPA templates
3. **Develop BUMDes capacity building programs** with private sector partnerships

### 11.3 SDG 13 Climate Action
1. **Quantify avoided emissions** from each PLTD conversion for carbon credit monetization
2. **Integrate climate resilience** into mini-grid design (flood-resistant, cyclone-resistant)
3. **Link electrification to NDC targets** with transparent MRV systems

## Conclusion

PLN's electrification of Indonesia's 3T villages represents one of the world's largest ongoing rural electrification efforts, combining grid extension, off-grid renewables, and diesel replacement. The **Lisdes Program (2025-2029)** and **Diesel Replacement Program** are the primary policy vehicles, targeting 10,068 villages and 5,200 diesel plants respectively. 

**Technological solutions** span solar home systems, solar mini-grids (30-110 kWp), micro-hydro (5-100 kW), and BESS-integrated systems. **Community ownership models** through BUMDes and organizations like IBEKA demonstrate higher sustainability but require capacity building and supportive regulatory frameworks.

**SDG Alignment** is strongest for **SDG 7** (99.8% electrification), with emerging impacts on **SDG 9** (infrastructure innovation through mini-grids and DRP tenders) and **SDG 13** (diesel substitution reducing ~515 kg CO2/household/year). However, the remaining 658,000 households in Eastern Indonesia's 3T regions require intensified focus on productive use of energy, financial sustainability, and community capacity building to ensure no one is left behind.

**Critical next steps** include standardizing 3T targeting, integrating climate resilience into infrastructure design, and developing viable business models that combine social equity with environmental sustainability.
